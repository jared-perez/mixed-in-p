Guide

How Mixed in P works

A plain-language tour of the app: what each part does, what problem it solves, and how to use it. No programming knowledge needed.

Want every setting and option spelled out? The reference docs cover the same ground in more detail.

What Mixed in P does

Mixed in P gets your tracks ready to play out. Drop in a folder of new music and it can:

  • Find the tempo, key and energy of every track.
  • Clean up the filenames, and add the tempo and key to them if you like.
  • Convert files to the format your gear wants, without ever making the quality worse than you asked for.
  • Tag them: fix artist, title, label, cover art and so on, by hand or from Discogs.
  • File them into playlists you can export to Rekordbox, Serato or Traktor.

It also has a full player with a loop slicer and metronome, a list of tracks that mix with the one playing, a piano for checking keys by ear, and a spectrum view for spotting fake-quality files.

It is a prep tool. It's for the hours before the gig, not for performing with.

A typical first session

  1. Drag a folder of new tracks onto the Analyze button in the sidebar. Analysis starts on its own.
  2. Watch the BPM, Key Code and Energy columns fill in.
  3. By default the app also writes the results into each file's tags and renames each file with its tempo and key, e.g. 128 8A - Track Name. If you'd rather look first, press Freeze before you drop (see Analyze).
  4. Drag the tracks onto the Player button. Play them, sort by key or BPM, and save the ones you want as a playlist.
  5. Right-click the playlist in the sidebar, choose Export…, and import it into your DJ software.

When you do this every week, the pipeline turns steps 1–4 into one button press.

Heads-up: out of the box, analysis changes your files. It writes BPM, key and energy tags, and it renames files. That's usually what you want, and renames can be undone from History. Every part of it can be switched off in Settings, or paused for a session with Freeze.

Harmonic mixing in 60 seconds

Two tracks blend smoothly when their keys are related. If they aren't, even a perfect beatmatch can sound sour where the basslines and pads overlap.

Key names like “F♯ minor” or “D♭ major” are hard to compare in your head if you aren't familiar with them, so Mixed in P gives every key a key code: a number from 1 to 12 plus a letter. A means minor and B means major. The numbers go round like a clock face.

The rule: a track mixes well with any track that has

8A → 8A
the same code
8A ↔ 8B
the same number, other letter: the same notes in a different mood
8A → 7A · 9A
one step either way on the same letter

So from 8A, your safe next picks are 8A, 8B, 7A and 9A.

Where you'll see this in the app:

  • Compatible Tracks in the Player finds these matches in your library for you.
  • The Keyboard panel lets you hear why: play two chords and compare.
  • Prefer note names (Am) or Traktor's Open Key (1m)? Change it in Settings → Notation.

The full table of codes is under Key codes.

Getting around

The sidebar

The buttons down the left switch pages, and they also take drops.

The pages, top to bottom: Player, Rename, Convert, Analyze, Metadata, Spectrum, Keyboard, then Settings and History at the bottom.

Drop files on a button to send them there.

You don't have to open a panel first. Drag files or whole folders from Finder or Explorer onto a button, and they go straight into that panel. Folders are searched all the way down.

  • Dropping on Player, Rename, Convert or Analyze adds the files and leaves you where you are, so you can keep working.
  • Dropping on Metadata or Spectrum opens that page with the file loaded.

You can drag rows between panels the same way.

Select rows in one panel and drop them on another panel's button.

  • Between Rename, Convert and Analyze, tracks move. They're a to-do list, so a track leaves one step when it goes to the next.
  • Out of the Player, tracks are always copied. A playlist only loses a track when you remove it on purpose.
  • Onto Metadata or Spectrum, a track is just opened for viewing.

Other things the sidebar shows you

  • The Convert icon spins and the Analyze magnifier looks around while they're working, so you can see progress from any page.
  • A small Auto badge on Analyze means dropped files are analyzed straight away.
  • Playlists (or Shift+Tab from anywhere) swaps the buttons for your playlist tree. The thin || button shows both side by side.

The header bar

  • Add: pick Files… or Folder… to add to the page you're looking at.
  • Playing: …: click it to jump to the playlist the current track is playing from.
  • Three small pipeline toggles and a Playlist name box. See The pipeline.
  • ? opens the About box. Click through its slides for a quick tour, or use Check for updates.

The pipeline

One button takes new tracks through Rename → Convert → Analyze and files them into a playlist.

Prepping a batch of new music is the same four chores every time: clean the names, convert, analyze, file it somewhere. The pipeline does them in one go, in the right order, and nothing gets missed.

How to use it

  1. Pick the steps you want. Rename, Convert and Analyze each have a small wave-in-a-triangle toggle next to their main button. The same three toggles appear in the header. Lit means the step is included.
  2. Name the target playlist in the Playlist name box in the header. Pick an existing playlist, or type a new name and one is created for you.
  3. Load the files into the first step you want and press Start Pipeline. That's the panel's main button, renamed while its step is on.

The files are processed step by step and land in the playlist. The run reports at the end: “Pipeline complete: 12 added to New Friday, 0 skipped, 0 errors.”

Good to know

  • The order is always Rename → Convert → Analyze. A run goes forward from the panel you started in, so starting in Convert never renames.
  • Every run ends in the playlist, so you don't need a toggle for that part.
  • Set up your rename and convert options before starting. The pipeline uses whatever is set in each panel.
  • Tracks that are already analyzed aren't analyzed again. They go straight to the playlist.
  • Files Convert can't take (see Convert) stay behind in Convert, with their status saying why. The rest carry on.
  • With Auto on and the Analyze step lit, dropping files onto Analyze starts a run. Files dropped mid-run join the run in progress. If no playlist is named yet, the files are only analyzed, and a notice tells you so.
  • Duplicates are skipped during a run rather than asking every time.

Rename

Clean up lots of filenames at once, with a live preview.

Downloads arrive as 01_artist-track_(original_mix)_promo.wav. Tidy names are easier to search in your DJ software and easier to read on a CDJ screen.

How to use it: drop files in, set the options, check the Preview column, and press Apply Rename.

The options (applied in this order)

  • Trim Start / Trim End: chop a number of characters off the front or back of every name, e.g. to strip 01_ track numbers or a _promo tag. The file extension is never touched.
  • Prepend Text / Append Text: add text to the start, the end, or both. Typing in a box switches it on.
  • Remove Underscores: Artist_Track → Artist Track.
  • Space Dashes: Artist-Track → Artist - Track. Some DJ software searches badly on names without spaces around the dash.
  • Clear resets everything.

Good to know

  • The preview shows the result before anything changes on disk.
  • A Conflict means two files would end up with the same name, or a file with that name already exists. Conflicting rows are skipped.
  • Renamed rows turn green and say Changed.
  • Undo Last puts the last batch back. Older batches can be undone from History. Undo also fixes the names in your playlists, so nothing goes missing.
  • Double-click a name to open it as text you can select and copy.

Automatic renaming after analysis

Separately from this panel, analysis can rename each file with its tempo and key as it finishes. This is on by default. Choose the style in Settings → Naming format:

StyleExample
BPM + key first (default)128 8A - Track Name
Key + BPM first8A 128 - Track Name
Key first8A - Track Name
Key + BPM lastTrack Name - 8A 128
Key lastTrack Name - 8A

Why do this? Your tempo and key then travel with the file into any app, any USB stick, and any folder view, sorted and readable at a glance.

If any new name in a batch would clash with an existing file, nothing in that batch is renamed, and a notice names the file that clashed.

Convert

Change files to WAV, FLAC, AIFF or MP3, without ever accidentally making them worse.

Your gear or your DJ software may want a particular format. Older CDJs, for example, can choke on FLAC or on 96 kHz/24-bit files. Convert makes the copies it needs. It also refuses conversions that would only pretend to improve quality, because those fill your drive for nothing.

How to use it

  1. Drop files in.
  2. Pick the Target Format. For WAV, FLAC or AIFF, also pick a Sample Rate and Bit Depth. For MP3, pick a Bitrate. Keep source leaves that setting as it is on each file, which is handy for a mixed batch.
  3. Choose where the new files go:
    • With the Source toggle lit, each new file is saved next to its original.
    • Otherwise it goes into a folder you pick.
  4. Check the Status column, then press Convert.

Your originals are never deleted or overwritten. Tags and cover art are copied over to the new files.

Reading the Status column

StatusMeaning
ReadyWill convert.
Same formatAlready this format at these settings. There's nothing to do unless you pick a lower rate, depth or bitrate.
Would upsampleYou asked for a higher sample rate or bit depth than the file has. Pick one no higher than the file's.
Lossy sourceMP3, M4A and OGG can't become WAV, FLAC or AIFF. An MP3 can only become a lower-bitrate MP3.
IncompleteThe file seems cut short, like a download that didn't finish. Re-download it.
Why “quality never goes up”: turning an MP3 into a WAV makes a file ten times bigger that sounds exactly like the MP3. The missing detail was thrown away when the MP3 was made and can't be put back. The same goes for “upsampling” 44.1 kHz to 96 kHz. Mixed in P stops you before you fill a drive with files that only look better.

Analyze

Finds each track's BPM, key and energy (1–10).

Knowing the tempo and key of every track is the base of beatmatching and harmonic mixing. Doing it by ear for a whole crate takes forever.

How to use it: drop files onto the panel, or onto its sidebar button.

  • With Auto on (the default), analysis starts immediately.
  • With it off, files wait as Pending until you press Analyze.

It takes a few seconds per track.

The columns

  • BPM, with a Conf (confidence) score.
  • Key and Key Code, with their own Conf.
  • Alt Keys: the next most likely keys and how likely they are. Useful when a track sits between two keys.
  • Energy: 1 (chilled) to 10 (peak time). It's worked out from loudness, how busy the rhythm is, and how bright the sound is.
  • Status: Pending → Analyzing → Analyzed, or Error.

What happens after each track

All of these are on by default, and all can be changed in Settings:

  • The BPM and key are written into the file's tags, so they show up in Rekordbox, Serato, Traktor and so on.
  • The energy is written into the comment (e.g. 6 - visit my webpage) and into its own tag field.
  • The file is renamed with its BPM and key (see Rename).

Freeze

Analyze without changing anything.

Press Freeze and analysis still fills in the table, but writes nothing to your files and renames nothing. Use it to try the app on music you don't want touched yet, or to check results before committing. It lasts until you press it again or restart the app.

Cancel

Cancel stops the batch right away. Tracks that already finished keep their results. Tracks that never started go back to Pending, so you can press Analyze to pick up where you left off.

Good to know

  • Half/double tempo. A 128 BPM track can just as easily be heard as 64 or 256. Analysis keeps results inside a DJ-friendly range, 99–199 BPM by default. If you play drum & bass, dubstep or downtempo, adjust Settings → Tempo Range so results land where you'd count them.
  • Low confidence is worth a listen. The History → Key History view flags tracks whose key or tempo was a close call.
  • WAV files can't hold tags. WAV rows are tinted and say Analyzed, no tags. The results are still shown, and the file can still be renamed with them. If you want tags, convert to AIFF or FLAC; both are just as lossless.

Player & playlists

Play your tracks, organise them into playlists, and see what mixes with what.

Playing

  • Double-click a track to play it. Space plays and pauses.
  • The Previous, Play / Pause, Stop and Next buttons and the Vol slider are pinned at the bottom.
  • Playback follows the playlist it started from, not whatever is on screen. You can browse other playlists or search while a set plays on.
  • The Playing: line at the bottom has two uses:
    • Drag it onto any playlist to add the current track.
    • Click In Playlist: … (or press Cmd/Ctrl+L) to jump back to where it's playing from.

Playlists

Show the tree with the sidebar's Playlists button, or press Shift+Tab from anywhere.

  • Scratch is your throwaway working list. It starts empty each launch, like a notepad. (Settings can keep it between sessions.) Save Playlist turns whatever is in it into a real playlist.
  • + Playlist and + Folder create new items. Drag them to reorder or to nest them inside folders.
  • Dropping tracks on a playlist adds copies. Nothing is removed from where it came from.
  • Removing tracks (Delete/Backspace) asks first in a saved playlist. Cmd+Backspace (Ctrl+Delete on Windows) skips the question. Removing never stops the music.
  • Cmd/Ctrl+Z undoes playlist edits, including adds, removes, clears and moves.
  • If a track is already in the playlist, you'll be asked whether to add it again. A set can repeat a track on purpose. Settings can make this always add or always skip.

Export to your DJ software

Right-click a playlist → Export… and choose .m3u8, .m3u or a Tracklist (.txt) for posting online. Then import it:

  • Rekordbox: File → Import Playlist.
  • Serato: drag the file onto the crate panel.
  • Traktor: File → Import.

Export and Copy Tracks… copies the audio files and a playlist into one folder. Zip it and send it, and it works on the other machine. Export Folder… exports a whole folder of playlists. Settings → Export All Playlists… makes a full backup.

When files have moved

Moved a folder, renamed a drive, or plugged your USB stick into a different port? Missing tracks show dimmed. Right-click one and choose Locate Missing File…:

  • Locate… lets you point at that one file.
  • Find in Folder… scans a folder and relinks every missing track it finds there, across all your playlists, in one go. It recognises files by their contents even if they were renamed.

Search

Type in the search box to search All playlists (the default) or This playlist. You can switch between them with the button beside the box.

  • Searching everything matches artist, title, album, filename, comment and key.
  • The # column shows how many playlists each result is in. Hover it to see which ones.
  • Select a result and the playlists holding it light up in the tree.
  • Esc leaves the search.

Columns and look

  • Right-click the column headers to show extra columns: Album, Genre, Track #, Label, Bitrate, Energy, Art, Date Added, Date Created, Format, Bit Depth.
  • Drag columns to reorder or resize them. The same menu has Fit … to Longest and Reset Columns.
  • Click a header to sort. A third click goes back to your own order.
  • Settings → Playlist Text Size and Playlist Artwork (how much of the cover the Art column shows) change how the list looks.

Editing tags in the list

Click a cell in an already-selected row to edit Artist, Title, BPM, Key, Comment or Year. It works like renaming in Finder. Changes are written straight into the file.

To avoid editing by accident while you browse, tick Edit Lock at the top right.

Cover art

Click the small cover next to the title to show a big version in the sidebar. Drop a JPG or PNG onto either cover to make it the playing track's artwork. (Edit Lock must be off.)

Compatible Tracks

“What can I play next?”, answered from your own library.

Click the Compatible Tracks button while a track is playing. A panel opens listing every track in your playlists that mixes with it, best first:

  1. Key: the same key first, then relative major/minor, then one step around the wheel.
  2. Tempo: within about 8%. Half- and double-time matches are included but marked, since they mix at a different feel.
  3. Energy: the closest energy wins ties, so you can hold the room steady.

Preview: press and hold the play icon beside a match to hear it. Sound stops when you move away, and clicking again skips ahead 30 seconds. Previewing stops the main player.

Double-click a match to add it to the list on screen.

It only knows about tracks that are in a playlist and have been analyzed.

Loop Slicer, waveforms & metronome

These live in the Player, in the collapsible row under the playlist. Each part has its own toggle, so you can open just what you need.

Waveform and Zoomed Wave

  • Waveform swaps the seek bar for the whole track's waveform. You can see the build-ups and breakdowns and click anywhere to jump.
  • Zoomed Wave is a close-up around the playhead. You can scrub it while paused to find an exact hit.
  • The colours are set in Settings → Waveform / Visuals:
    • Solid: one colour.
    • Loudness: brighter where it's louder.
    • Frequency bands: bass, mids and highs tinted differently, so kicks, vocals and hats stand out.
    • Tone: coloured by how bright or dark the sound is.

Loop Slicer

Cut a loop or sample out of a track and save it as its own file.

For lifting a loop, an acapella phrase, or a drum hit into your sampler or DAW without opening a whole production app.

How to use it

  1. Play the track. Press Q (or Mark) at the start point and E at the end point. You can also drag the markers on the waveform.
  2. Fine-tune with the 10 ms nudge buttons, or type a Length.
  3. Switch on Loop (L) to hear it loop.
  4. Hold S to audition from the start marker. It jumps back when you let go.
  5. Name it under Save Slice As, pick a format, and press Slice.

The slice is saved next to the original by default. Nothing is ever overwritten.

Mark on beat: switch this on and your marks, jumps and loop starts snap to the metronome's next click. With the metronome set to the track's tempo, your loops land on the beat without fiddly nudging. Switching it on opens the metronome and loads the track's BPM, but you start the click yourself.

The keys Q, E, S and L only work while the slicer is open and you aren't typing in a box.

Metronome

A click track for checking a tempo or practising along.

  • Start / Stop.
  • Set the tempo by any of:
    • typing it;
    • dragging the BPM box up or down (hold Shift for fine steps);
    • using − and +;
    • Tap along;
    • Track, which takes the loaded track's tempo.
  • The ‹ › bend buttons nudge the beat back or forward while held, like pushing a platter.
  • Pick a click sound (silent light, standard or higher-pitched) and a volume. The first beat of each bar is accented.
  • Global Click keeps the click going when you switch to another page.

Visuals

Eye candy that moves to the music, either behind the playlist or in its own window.

Click the eye button in the Player and pick one. Every visual can play behind the playlist. All but Waveform and Smoke also come as a Popout in their own window, which is good for a second screen. Visuals off turns them off.

Metadata & Discogs

View and edit one file's tags and cover art.

Good tags are how your DJ software sorts, searches and displays your music. Most promos arrive with missing or messy ones.

How to use it: drop a file on the panel or its sidebar button, or right-click a Player track → Open in Metadata Panel.

  • Edit Title, Artist, Album, Label, Genre, BPM, Key, Year, Track # or Comment. Each field saves as soon as you click away; there's no Save button.
  • Clearing a field removes that tag from the file.
  • Need a field that isn't shown? Pick it from Add field….
  • Cover art: drop a JPG or PNG onto the art box, or use Add Artwork…. Click the cover to see it large; right-click to remove it.
  • Reload re-reads the file if something else changed it. Eject closes it.
  • Open File Location shows the file in Finder or Explorer.

Good to know: WAV files can't store these tags. Use AIFF or FLAC if you want tags on a lossless file.

Discogs lookup (optional)

Fill in missing details and cover art from Discogs, and approve every change before it's written.

Typing label, year and catalogue details by hand for every promo is tedious, and a cover makes a track easy to spot in your library.

Setting it up (once)

  1. Go to Settings → Online Metadata and tick Look up track details online (Discogs).
  2. Click Get a Token…, which opens Discogs in your browser. Create a free personal token there and paste it in.

It's off by default, and the app makes no internet requests until you turn it on. A lookup sends only the artist and title, never your audio or your library. BPM, key and energy always come from Mixed in P's own analysis.

Using it

  • In Metadata, click Look Up Online….
  • A review window shows Current beside From Discogs. Tick what you want and click Apply:
    • Empty fields come pre-ticked.
    • Fields you've already filled in are not ticked, so nothing gets overwritten unless you choose it.
  • Wrong pressing? Pick another from Select release.
  • Wrong track? Pick the right one from Select track.
  • Lots of tracks? Select them in the Player, right-click → Look Up Online…, and review each in turn.

The Discogs tab remembers which release a file was tagged from and shows its full details offline next time: label, catalogue number, tracklist, credits and more. Click the small arrow beside any value to copy it into the tags.

Find Cover Online… is for when the tags are fine but the cover is missing. It lets you borrow the cover from any pressing of the release.

Keyboard

A piano for hearing how keys fit together.

The key-code rules are easier to trust once you've heard them. Play the chord of one track's key, then a compatible one, then an incompatible one, and you'll hear the difference.

How to use it

  • Click the keys, or play them on your computer keyboard:
    • White keys: A S D F G H J K L ;
    • Black keys: W E T Y U O P
  • Z / X move down or up an octave.
  • Minor Chord / Major Chord chooses which kind of chord each key plays.
  • Each piano key is labelled with its key code.

The key wheel (the strip and the Hex Grid or Circle of Fifths view) lights up the compatible keys while you hold a chord. Click any segment to hear it. The info box spells out the key and what it mixes with.

Spectrum

A picture of a file's frequencies, for spotting fake or low-quality files.

A “WAV” or “320 MP3” from a sketchy source is sometimes a low-quality MP3 in disguise. You can't always hear it on laptop speakers, but you'll hear it on a club system.

How to read it: drop a file on the panel. Time runs left to right, pitch from low (bottom) to high (top), and colour shows loudness.

  • A real lossless or high-quality file usually has content reaching near the top.
  • A low-bitrate file shows a hard, flat ceiling where everything above a certain height is cut off. Around 16 kHz is typical of 128 kbps.

Split Screen puts two files side by side, e.g. the “WAV” you bought next to a known-good file. Sensitivity adjusts how much quiet detail is shown.

History

  • Rename History lists every rename batch. Select one and press Undo Selected to put those names back.
  • Key History lists recently analyzed tracks and their results, with a note when a key or tempo was a close call.
  • Export CSV saves either list for a spreadsheet.

Settings

Settings save as you change them. Language and theme apply after a restart.

  • Language: 12 languages.
  • Theme: Nuevo Leon, Dark Mode, Daylight, Slate or Neon Dark.
  • Default Audio Player: make Mixed in P open when you double-click an audio file.
  • Waveform / Visuals: colour mode, colour, and half or full waveform.
  • Playlist Text Size / Playlist Artwork: how the Player list looks.
  • Tempo Range: the BPM range analysis aims for (default 99–199).
  • After analysis: whether to auto-analyze on drop, write BPM and key tags, rename files, and the Naming format.
  • Notation: key codes (8A), note names (Am) or Open Key (1m).
  • Energy Tag: whether and how energy goes into the comment, and whether it gets its own tag field.
  • Online Metadata: the Discogs switch and token.
  • Playlists: the duplicates prompt, keeping Scratch between sessions, full vs relative paths in exports, and Export All Playlists….
  • Troubleshooting: Show Log File finds the app's log for you, to attach to a bug report.
  • Reset to Default: puts everything back, apart from language, theme and window layout.

Open with Mixed in P

Right-click audio files in Finder or Explorer → Open With → Mixed in P, or make it your default player in Settings. The files go into Scratch and the first one starts playing straight away, like any music player. Your saved playlists are never touched by this.

Questions & fixes

The BPM is half or double what I expected.

Change Settings → Tempo Range so it covers the tempos you actually play, then re-analyze.

The key looks wrong.

Check Alt Keys: the runner-up is often right on tracks with ambiguous harmony. Cleared the Analyze results already? The same Alt Keys are kept in History → Key History. Fix the key in Metadata, or inline in the Player.

My tags didn't show up in my DJ software.

Is it a WAV? WAV can't store tags. Otherwise, was Freeze on? Some DJ apps also need you to re-read the file's tags after a change.

I renamed files by mistake.

History → Rename History → Undo Selected.

A track shows as dimmed / missing.

The file moved. Right-click → Locate Missing File… → Find in Folder….

Convert won't convert a file.

Read its Status. It's almost always a lossy file or a setting higher than the source.

Sound comes out of the wrong device.

The app uses whichever output was selected when it launched. Switch outputs first, then restart Mixed in P.

Something went wrong and I want to report it.

Go to Settings → Troubleshooting → Show Log File, and attach that file to your report on GitHub.
